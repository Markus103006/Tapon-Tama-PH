TAPON TAMA PH / PROJECT INFORMATION

Disclaimers & image credits

Educational purpose

Tapon Tama PH is an independent student advocacy project for MO-ENV076, People and the Earth’s Ecosystems. It aims to inform readers about PPE waste and encourage concern for people and ecosystems. It is not an official government campaign or a source of medical advice.

Images and illustration

The homepage image showing a hand holding a mask above a waste bin is AI-generated and reused from our Tapon Tama PH project storyboard. It illustrates a scenario and is not a photograph documenting an actual person, household, event, or research finding. It does not demonstrate an approved disposal method.

The project logo was supplied for this project. Its circular emblem and wordmark have been prepared as separate raster assets. The image and logo are not evidence supporting the research claims.

Research and dates

Sources are linked alongside the relevant claims. The 7-in-10 figure refers to participating LGUs in a pandemic-era study reported in 2021. It is not a current national statistic or a finding about every LGU in the Philippines. The companion study provides the broader research context.

Health and disposal information

Used PPE is not automatically infectious. Contamination, setting, and handling matter. This project does not prescribe one disposal rule for all households and healthcare facilities. Consult current guidance from the appropriate local authorities and healthcare providers for your situation.

Campaign wording and rights

“Tapon nang tama. Protektado ang pamilya at komunidad.” expresses the campaign’s aspiration. It is not a guarantee that a particular action prevents illness or environmental harm. Philippine-inspired visual elements do not imply government endorsement.

Referenced publications belong to their respective authors or organizations. Links and credits identify sources; they do not imply endorsement or permission to reproduce material beyond its applicable terms.

01 / Start with the everyday

Your bin is not
the end of the story.

PPE waste includes discarded masks, gloves, gowns, and face shields. It is only part of healthcare waste, not all of it.

We look at what happens after use in Philippine households and healthcare settings. The concern is not protection itself, but waste that is mixed, poorly handled, or released into the environment.

03 Our environment

The air, water, and habitats we all depend on.

The evidence points to gaps in segregation and waste-management systems, not only individual disposal choices (Tanchuling, 2021). WHO notes that poorly managed healthcare waste can expose handlers and communities to health risks (WHO, 2024). Research outside the Philippines has documented animals becoming entangled in or ingesting PPE litter (Hiemstra et al., 2021). Necessary protection still matters; the concern is how waste is managed after use.

What this number does and does not mean

This finding comes from a pandemic-era study published in 2021; the published source does not state the exact data-collection dates or the number of participating LGUs. It describes the LGUs in that study, not every LGU in the Philippines, and is not a current national PPE-waste total.
